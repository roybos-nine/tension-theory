# Tension Theory — Physical System Analysis

## Purpose

Use Tension Theory as a lens for analyzing physical systems — and as a decoder ring between standard physics vocabulary and TT primitives.

TT is itself a physical theory. This skill is not a metaphor — it is a structural mapping.
Use it to analyze physical phenomena through TT primitives, and to identify regimes where TT predictions diverge from continuum models.

---

## Vocabulary (Standard Physics → TT)

**Mass:** Update cost per node. Resistance to state change — the tension required to modify a node's local state per unit of update.

**Energy:** Tension redistribution capacity. Not a substance stored in a node, but a measure of available update potential across the local graph.

**Force:** A tension gradient driving updates between adjacent nodes. Not a thing that acts — a difference that compels.

**Entropy:** Accumulated irreversibility. The amount of prior state inaccessible due to finite memory overwrites and asymmetric update cost.

**Temperature:** Average update frequency and cost across a subsystem. High temperature = high update rate = rapid tension redistribution.

**Particle:** A stable tension loop — a persistent subgraph maintaining internal tension circulation that resists dispersion (Section 5.4). Identity is the pattern, not the substrate.

**Field:** A distributed tension gradient across a graph region — not an entity, but a statistical regularity of aggregate update dynamics (Section 5.5).

**Wave:** A propagating tension update through a stable relay chain of nodes.

**Gravity:** A systematic tension gradient produced by persistent, dense local configurations; affects update propagation rates in surrounding regions.

**Spacetime curvature:** Non-uniform tension distribution affecting how quickly updates propagate — not a geometric property of a background, but an emergent regularity of dense persistent configurations (Section 6.2).

**Superposition:** Unresolved local tension across multiple candidate update paths — not a physical mixture, but an unforced state awaiting coupling (Section 6.1).

**Entanglement:** Shared update history encoded in graph structure. Correlated states arising from prior coupling — not ongoing non-local influence (Section 6.1).

**Wavefunction collapse:** An irreversible state update under memory constraint during coupling between two subsystems (Section 6.1). Not a special rule — it is an update.

**Observation:** A physical interaction redistributing tension between observer and system nodes. Not passive — it has cost and changes both states (Section 5.7).

## Analysis Algorithm

### Step 1 — Define the Physical System as a Graph

Identify:

- Interacting entities (nodes)
- Interaction channels (edges)
- Update timescale (what counts as one step in this system?)

---

### Step 2 — Identify Memory Constraints

For each node ask:

- What state can it retain?
- At what precision?
- What is the effective memory horizon (how far back does history matter)?
- What is overwritten or compressed at each update?

---

### Step 3 — Map Tension Distribution

Determine:

- Where are the high-tension edges?
- What drives the gradient (incompatible states, scarcity, density)?
- Is tension distributed smoothly (field regime) or concentrated in stable loops (particle regime)?

---

### Step 4 — Model the Update Dynamics

Ask:

- What is the local update rule?
- What is conserved across updates (tension totals, graph symmetries)?
- What is irreversible (what cannot be reconstructed from the current state)?
- What is the fan-out of each update (how many neighbors are affected)?

---

### Step 5 — Identify Regime

Classify which approximation applies:

- Classical (smooth tension gradients, high node count, slow topology change — continuum description valid)
- Quantum (unresolved multi-path tension, low memory per node, update history matters — discrete treatment required)
- Relativistic (update propagation delay dominant — causal order becomes the primary constraint)
- Extreme (memory bounds become physically relevant — TT predictions diverge from continuum models)

---

## Testable Predictions (Paper, Section 7)

Use these as a checklist when analyzing systems near the limits of continuum models:

- [ ] **Irreversibility lower bound:** Isolated, high-precision systems will exhibit fundamental reversibility limits beyond standard thermodynamics
- [ ] **Locality of influence:** No experiment can exploit entanglement to transmit information without local interaction history
- [ ] **Memory-limited precision:** Physical constants and measurable quantities exhibit discretization consistent with bounded state representation
- [ ] **Observation back-action:** Measurement disturbance exceeds standard quantum limits in memory-overwrite regimes
- [ ] **Continuum breakdown:** Deviations from continuum-based predictions appear in extreme regimes without invoking quantum gravity
- [ ] **Simulation equivalence:** No physically realizable process requires non-local or infinite-memory computation

## Output Format

Always produce:

1. System graph (node and edge types, interaction topology)
2. Tension distribution map (gradients, stable loops, high-cost edges)
3. Memory constraints (precision limits, decoherence timescale, history depth)
4. Update dynamics (what evolves, what is conserved, what is irreversible)
5. Regime classification
6. Applicable TT predictions for this system and regime
